Biography

Zhuang Liu (publishing under the handle lyeutsaon) is an independent street and urban documentary photographer based in the San Francisco Bay Area, California.

Working primarily across San Francisco, San Jose, and Los Angeles on the US West Coast alongside ongoing work in Shanghai, his photographs examine nocturnal light, urban velocity, candid street theater, and the psychological tension of contemporary metropolitan life. Moving between high-contrast monochrome, slow-shutter kinetic blur, and direct flash, his projects sequence fleeting street encounters into long-form visual essays.

Selected Photography Series

  • I Tear Up the Bay When I Come Through
    Nocturnal odyssey across San Francisco Bay Area transit cars, fog ridgelines, street takeovers, and blinding urban light.
  • I Do Not Care If We Go Down in History as Barbarians
    Long-standing documentary project walking the pavements of San Francisco and San Jose to record the angst, resilience, and unseen human reality of the modern Bay Area.
  • Aerobatic Activities
    Color street photography catching West Coast street culture mid-flip—loud color, nerve, and sudden street theater.
  • Das Gespenst
    Spectral visual ethnography of West Coast streets, pairing everyday gestures with quiet magical realism.
  • Hyperstars Sucked Into The Hypermassive
    Dystopian monochrome sequence set amid San Francisco's Market Street, Union Square, Salesforce Tower, and Bay Bridge, pairing kinetic light with Chinese-language prose on technological alienation.
  • Self Portraits and Behind the Scenes
    Reflections, lighting apparatus studies, and candid portraits of the photographer at work.
